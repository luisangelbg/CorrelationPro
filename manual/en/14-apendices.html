AThe files the app reads and writes

CorrelationPro runs in the browser and sends nothing to any server: everything that goes in and out is a file on your computer or the clipboard. This appendix says which ones.

iWhat the browser remembers

The preferences—the language, light or dark mode, the palette, the style of the numbers, the typeface of the figures, your own palette—are kept in the browser’s storage, on your computer. The data are not: they are lost when you close the tab, unless you save the project.

BThe reading rules, gathered

The scales and cutoffs the app uses to put a word or a color next to a number, in one place, so you can cite and discuss them.

!Almost all of these cutoffs are customs, not laws

The .05 of significance, the .30 of a “medium” correlation, the .60 of the KMO or the 3 of the Bayes factor are reading conventions, not thresholds that come out of a theory. Use them to find your bearings and always say which one you used; what you know about the phenomenon outweighs any table.

CGlossary

Each English term is followed by its Spanish equivalent, the one the app shows when its interface is in Spanish.

Attenuation (atenuación)
The reduction of a correlation caused by the measurement error of its variables. It is corrected with their reliabilities (Section 12.3).
Autocorrelation (autocorrelación)
The resemblance of a variable to itself at nearby times or places. It reduces the independent information in the data.
BCa (BCa)
Bootstrap interval corrected for bias and acceleration (Efron 1987).
Bootstrap (bootstrap)
Resampling the cases with replacement and recomputing the statistic many times to learn its variation.
Centered log-ratio (log-cociente centrado)
The logarithm of each part of a composition divided by the geometric mean of all of them (Aitchison 1986).
Collider (colisionador)
A variable that is a consequence of two others. Controlling for it creates a correlation that does not exist.
Confidence · credible · prediction interval (intervalo de confianza · creíble · de predicción)
The range of values compatible with the data (frequentist), the one that contains a percentage of the posterior (Bayesian), and the place where the value of a new study would fall (meta-analysis).
Copula (cópula)
The shape of the dependence between two variables, separated from their marginal distributions.
Distance correlation (correlación de distancias)
A measure of dependence that is zero, in the population, only when the variables are independent.
Ecological fallacy (falacia ecológica)
Drawing conclusions about individuals from the correlation between groups (Robinson 1950).
Effective sample size (tamaño de muestra efectivo)
The number of independent cases that data with autocorrelation are worth.
Eigenvalue (valor propio)
The variance carried by each component of a matrix. Their sum is the number of variables.
Fixed effect · random effects (efecto fijo · efectos aleatorios)
In a meta-analysis, assuming that all studies estimate the same thing, or that each one estimates its own value around an average.
Graphical lasso (lazo gráfico)
An estimate of the partial correlations that sets the weak ones to zero (Friedman, Hastie and Tibshirani 2008).
Heteroscedasticity (heterocedasticidad)
A spread that changes along the line; in the scatterplot it looks like a fan.
ICC (ICC)
The intraclass correlation coefficient: the part of the variation due to the cases and not to the raters.
Lin’s concordance (concordancia de Lin)
A measure of agreement that penalizes both the lack of correlation and the shifts in location and scale.
LOWESS (LOWESS)
A smoother that fits weighted local lines, to see the shape of a scatterplot without assuming it (Cleveland 1979).
Masking (enmascaramiento)
When several outliers inflate the mean and the covariance and the classical distance stops seeing them.
Modularity (modularidad)
How much more the nodes connect within their communities than would be expected by chance.
Monotonic (monótona)
A relationship that always goes up or always goes down, even if it is not straight.
Multiple testing (pruebas múltiples)
Running many tests at once. Without correction, some come out significant by chance alone.
Outlier (atípico)
A case far away from the others. It may be an error or a real value; the robust distance finds it even when there are several (Section 3.4).
p-value (valor p)
The probability of obtaining a result as extreme as the one observed if there were no relationship. It is not the probability that there is none.
Partial · semipartial correlation (correlación parcial · semiparcial)
The correlation that remains after removing the linear effect of other variables from both variables (partial) or from only one (semipartial).
Permutation (permutación)
Shuffling one variable against the other to learn the distribution of a statistic when there is no relationship.
Polychoric · tetrachoric · polyserial (policórica · tetracórica · poliserial)
The correlation of the latent continuous variables assumed to lie behind two ordinal variables, two binary ones, or an ordinal and a continuous one.
Positive definite (definida positiva)
A matrix whose eigenvalues are all greater than zero. A valid correlation matrix is one.
Prewhitening (preblanqueo)
Filtering two series with the autoregressive model of one of them before correlating them, to remove the memory that creates spurious correlations.
Pseudo-observations (pseudo-observaciones)
The ranks of each variable divided by n + 1: what is left of the data when the marginals are removed.
Range restriction (rango restringido)
Having observed only part of the span of a variable, which lowers the correlation.
Reliability (fiabilidad)
The part of the variation of a measure that is not error. It is estimated with an α, a test–retest or an ICC.
Simpson’s paradox (paradoja de Simpson)
A relationship that changes sign when different groups are pooled (Simpson 1951).
Suppression (supresión)
When controlling for a third variable makes a correlation grow or changes its sign.

DWhen something goes wrong

What you seeWhat it usually is and what to do
The columns come out stuck together in a single one, or the numbers as textThe separator or the decimal mark were not detected correctly. Choose them in Reading options in Block 2.
A value very far from the others, such as −999It is a missing-value code. Add it in Other missing codes and read the file again.
A variable does not appear in a blockIts kind or its role are not the ones that block uses. Check them in Block 2: almost all blocks take only the columns with the variable role.
“Too few complete pairs.”The two variables have missing values in different rows. Look at the missing-value pattern in Block 2.
The matrix is not positive definitePairwise missing values, a robust coefficient or more variables than rows. Section 5.5.
The distance correlation or τ* are not in the tableThey have size limits: distance correlation, 3,000 pairs; τ*, 80.
A computation takes a long timeMany rows, many resamples or a costly measure. Lower the resamples while you explore and raise them for the final result.
MIC says that only a 2 × 2 grid fitsThere are too few data for MIC. Use distance correlation or ξ.
The network changes a lot when one variable is removedThere are few rows per variable. Interpret only the strong edges.
The report does not openThe browser blocked the new tab. Use Download the report (HTML) or allow pop-up windows for the app.
The table does not paste with formattingThe browser did not grant clipboard permission. Select the table with the mouse and copy it, or download it as HTML.
“it is not a project of this app”The file is not a .correlationpro.json, or it was edited by hand.
I closed the tab and lost the analysisThe data are not saved automatically. Save the project in Block 13 before closing.
The text looks different on another computerThe app uses the system fonts, which change from one computer to another. Figures exported as PDF or PNG no longer change.

EThe tool: license and how to cite it

E.1 · What it is and what it is not

CorrelationPro is a program written from scratch: the computing engines—the coefficients, the tests, the matrices, the copulas, the networks, the meta-analysis—, the spreadsheet reader, the figures and the palettes are in its own code, which can be read. It uses no third-party computing libraries and downloads nothing when it opens. Its results were compared with those of reference programs—19,334 comparisons with no disagreement, Section I.9—, which served only for validation and are not part of the program.

Nor does it bring third-party fonts or icons: the typefaces are those of the computer, and the icons of the navigator and of the figure studio were drawn for the author’s suite. The only things that are not the author’s are the nine example datasets, which are in the public domain, and each one says why (Section I.7); the meta-analysis example is simulated and says so when it loads. The file PROCEDENCIA.md, in the program folder, says where each part comes from.

E.2 · License

It is distributed under the GNU General Public License, version 3 or later (GPL-3.0-or-later). The full text is in the LICENSE file, in the program folder.

iLicensing is not giving up authorship

Choosing a free license is a decision about what whoever receives the program may do. Authorship is neither transferred nor waived. You may use the program, study it, modify it and redistribute it; what the license asks in return is that, if you distribute a modified version, you do so with the same freedoms.

E.3 · How to cite it

The How to cite section of the home page gives the citation ready to copy, in social-sciences format and as a bibliography entry. The short form, for the program and for this manual:

∑

In the methods section, also cite the works behind the methods you used: the coefficient, the test, the correction, the model. Block 13 gathers them on its own, and only those your analysis actually used, which is exactly the list that has to go there.
